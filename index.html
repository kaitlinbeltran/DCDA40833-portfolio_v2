Semester Reflection

Overview

Over the course of this semester, I have developed a much stronger understanding of how digital tools, data, and design intersect in digital culture and data analytics. At the beginning of the semester, I had limited experience building websites or working with visualization tools beyond Excel, Google Sheets, and minimal Tableau. Through the labs in this course, I learned how to critically evaluate AI tools, analyze and critique visualizations, explore datasets using Tableau, and experiment with AI-assisted coding. My skillset has expanded tremendously.

The portfolio I created for this course reflects my development across several different technical and analytical skills. While each lab grew more detailed and difficult at times, they challenged me to exercise skills I hadn’t had before and truly understand each step of the coding process.

Proficiencies

Digital Culture

Throughout the semester, I developed a stronger understanding of how digital tools influence the way information is created, interpreted, and shared. In Lab 2, I evaluated multiple AI tools (ChatGPT and Canva Magic) using a structured framework that considered capabilities, appropriate use, ethical concerns, and transparency. This exercise helped me think critically about the role of AI in academic and professional environments, rather than simply using it as a productivity tool. In fact, I was actually shocked to find some inaccuracies and limitations of the tools (such as Canva Magic producing a presentation with false data), reminding me that not all digital tools are to be immediately trusted.

In Lab 5, I explored AI-assisted coding by adding a dark mode feature to my portfolio website. This lab helped me understand both the benefits and risks of “vibe coding,” where AI generates code based on natural language prompts. By documenting my level of understanding, I learned how important it is to maintain transparency and comprehension when working with AI-generated outputs, as it can be risky to rely too heavily on AI tools and therefore lack understanding.

Data Analytics

This course also strengthened my ability to analyze and interpret data through visualization. In Lab 3, I critiqued a data visualization using Edward Tufte’s framework, focusing on information resolution and identifying potential issues such as overreaching or cherry-picking. This exercise helped me recognize how visual design choices can influence how audiences interpret data.

In Lab 4, I used Tableau Public to explore a dataset and identify patterns in customer purchasing behavior. By selecting appropriate dimensions and measures, I was able to analyze trends and identify both expected patterns and surprising outliers. This process helped me understand how visualization tools can reveal patterns that are not immediately visible when looking at raw data alone. An important takeaway from this lab was that my first time around attempting it, I got feedback that I lacked a description of the dataset, its context, and where it came from. This was a huge lesson learned for me, as the dataset description is crucial to reading and understanding any data. I went back and corrected my work and recognized how important this lesson truly was.

Project Design and Planning

Another important skill I developed this semester was the ability to plan and execute digital projects that combine both design and technical components. In Lab 6, I created an interactive hometown map using a custom Mapbox basemap along with Python tools such as Folium and Pandas (tools of which I was originally pretty unfamiliar). This project required understanding how all of these different concepts tied together.

I also had to troubleshoot technical challenges such as file paths, zoom levels, and layer styling to ensure the final map functioned correctly. This process reinforced the importance of planning and problem-solving when working on digital projects. Because it contained both visual and technical components, this was my favorite lab by far.

Collaboration

Although all of the assignments were completed individually, collaboration still played an important role in the learning process. Throughout the semester, class discussions and peer feedback helped me refine my ideas and gave me new insights into approaching problems I ran into.

In particular, I compared my Lab 4 visualization with a peer. After analyzing her work, I realized that there were even more components I could add to my visual (such as color coding or a key). I learned so much from discussing the labs and seeing how others approached instructions and problems.

Self Evaluation

Over the course of the semester, I developed stronger technical and analytical skills through building my Skills Portfolio and completing each lab. Early in the course, I was unfamiliar with tools like GitHub, HTML, and CSS, but through the process of creating and revising my portfolio, I became more comfortable structuring web pages, embedding visualizations, and documenting my work clearly with comments. To be completely honest, the first day of class felt like it was being taught in a completely different language, whereas now I find myself not only reading, but also developing and understanding code. The labs also allowed me to connect course concepts to real-world contexts, such as in Lab 2, when I was able to evaluate AI tools through the lens of a sales representative at RealPage (the role I am entering after graduation), and in Lab 3, where I critically analyzed how data visualizations can shape the interpretation of information.

There were also areas where my work could have been stronger. In particular, the feedback I received on Lab 4 pointed out that my original visualization lacked sufficient explanation about the dataset and its source, which made the analysis difficult to evaluate. That feedback helped me recognize how important context and transparency are when presenting data, and I actually went back and updated the Lab to contain much more context and explanation of the dataset itself. Additionally, Lab 6 was submitted late because I became overwhelmed while trying to understand how the different technical and visual components fit together. Although I ultimately completed the work, this experience showed me that I could have managed my time better or asked for help sooner.

Grade

Based on my overall work this semester, I believe I have earned an A- in this course. My portfolio demonstrates consistent effort, improvement, and engagement with course concepts across multiple assignments. I approached many labs by applying them to real-world contexts and revising my work carefully to create a stronger final portfolio. The completed portfolio reflects my growth, and the key for me was approaching each assignment with genuine curiosity.

However, I also know that my work was definitely not perfect. As addressed earlier, my first Lab 4 submission lacked sufficient explanation of the dataset and design choices, and Lab 6 was submitted late because I struggled to integrate the components of the assignment. I did, however, go back, update my work, and ingrain every lesson from those assignments so that I don’t make the same mistakes again. Even with these challenges, I believe my overall work demonstrates strong learning, thoughtful reflection, and meaningful improvement throughout the semester, which is why I believe an A- accurately reflects my performance.
